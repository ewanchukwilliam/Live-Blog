# Is Neovim truly enough for professional development?

## Why Neovim?

I have ADHD. I may have jumped the gun a little early into my development journey. I did some research and came across Neovim. People swore it made you faster somehow. I really liked the idea of being closer to the root of development (i.e. the terminal). That and the fact that everything was customizably minimal to exactly what you need and no more really appealed to me. It was an extremely long term investment I'm still glad I made.

There's been another overarching reason: it gamified my development. Every challenge (which there were many) was an opportunity to level up my skills. Whether it's making my git integration more seamless, my LSP navigations more complete, finding a new yazi file manager plugin to speed up basic terminal operations, or wrapping REST requests faster. Everything is a game. You can level up and make anything faster and that idea still appeals to me.

## The problem?

Neovim is a strong editor. I've proven my capabilities with it. But the issues surrounding the open source environment have bubbled to the surface one too many times lately. I want to give it a final stress test.

I know Java. I've worked in the language often in my classes. But I avoid building projects with it due to Neovim's limited support. There are obviously better choices for Java development. There's JetBrains IntelliJ or even Eclipse IDE.

## A fork in the road

This is where I need to decide if I keep going all in on Neovim or start learning a JetBrains IDE for Java development. If I keep putting my eggs in this basket and it screws me in the future, I'd like to get a head start on the familiarizing process. Neovim has been good to me until now in an abusive open source compatibility maintenance sense, but I do have a strong workflow I enjoy from the terminal. I need to know for certain if this is it.

If Neovim cannot handle a basic real world Spring Boot backend for the ecommerce app I'm developing for my sister, then I need to start diverging and learning something else. If it's not as flexible as I was hoping then the quirks of open source aren't worth the trouble. It would be time to start simplifying my configurations to a more vim-like setup for the future. If Neovim cannot handle this backend then it's going to continue eating into valuable time that could have been better spent just building websites and gaining experience. I'm a fan of exponential development and if it cannot do Java then it is not exponential.

Sad to put my foot down but the writing has been on the wall for a while now. Its stink is all over r/neovim if you just read around. A lot of developers are allowed to use it as an IDE but they're in very specific roles. It gives me the "I use Arch BTW" vibes of editor choices. I'd rather not lose the employment race before starting so if I need to simplify sooner rather than later then I probably should.

## Mistakes from my last internship

I was given the freedom to use whatever IDE I wanted which was amazing but it came at an unforeseen cost. I didn't realize how open to mentorship my boss really was. I had the expectation that I was going to be spamming boilerplate code throughout my internship. I expected to just be shoved in a closet and do the menial labour. But my boss wanted to be my mentor. I really didn't see it as a possibility in my mind with how incredibly busy he was all the time. I just wanted to be fast and reliable so he didn't have to ever stall or wait or step back on my behalf.

He did actually want to help me grow as a developer. He was willing to slow down for my sake just to make sure I was able to learn things properly. It didn't register with me till way late into the internship that he was capable of all of those things. I was so busy trying to get faster I didn't realize I should have focused more on being an easier to mentor student.

I didn't see that my choice in editor got in the way of that. Every time there was a debugging issue or some obscure development issue like global find and replace, it was always different for me. I needed to basically drop out of the meeting, build the solution or configure it at least, and then rejoin hours later once it's ready.

Obviously it was a barrier to helping me. I even started to avoid one-on-ones so I could keep my development workflow and not have to answer for its shortcomings. That's what's inspiring this divergence.

## I won't make the same mistake twice

I can't imagine a world in which I couldn't just develop with vim on its own. That one seems much more globally compatible. Solving all of my own problems has served me well but I lost the opportunity to grow in lots of ways by just being more open minded.

If need be I'm absolutely willing to migrate over completely to a new IDE. Whether it's JetBrains for everything if vim doesn't pan out, or even just VSCode. I will make the move. I will not make my past mistakes again. If I cannot handle a simple Spring Boot project then I will move on.

No rugrats.
